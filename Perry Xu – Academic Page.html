About me

I’m a student in Engineering Science at the University of Toronto (B.A.Sc., 2025–2029), where I work at the boundary of optics, electronics, and software. I like building the whole experiment: the optical setup, the custom PCBs and firmware that drive it, and the simulation and analysis code that makes sense of the data.

I’m currently a research intern in the Li Qian Group (Nonlinear & Quantum Optics) and a team member at the University of Toronto Quantum Club, where I build NV-diamond magnetometry hardware. My projects range from a photonic circuit simulator to a high-dynamic-range optical power meter.

Research interests: nonlinear and quantum optics, fiber photonics, NV-center quantum sensing, precision instrumentation, photonic simulation.

Research & experience

Research Intern, Li Qian Group (Nonlinear & Quantum Optics)

University of Toronto · March 2026 – present

  • Constructed a fiber-based interferometer with electro-optic modulation to measure the optical nonlinearity of a fiber-based nonlinear material, using a piezo phase modulator to account for environmental phase drift.
  • Wrote Python scripts to automate the interferometer setup, preparing and extracting noisy oscilloscope waveform data through SCPI and processing it to characterize the nonlinearity.
  • Designed and built an STM32-based controller PCB for a high-voltage supply with programmable output over time up to 15 kV at 8 V resolution (~0.05% of full scale).
  • Designed and ran charge dynamics and optical simulations to model experimental processes and characterize observed phenomena, automating sweeps and data processing in Python.

Team Member, University of Toronto Quantum Club

University of Toronto · June 2026 – present

  • Built a PCB-scale NV-diamond vector magnetometer using optically initialized, microwave-driven spin qubits read out through laser-induced fluorescence; accepted for presentation at SPIE Photonics West.
  • Designed and simulated a broadband antenna in Ansys HFSS to drive NV ground-state spin transitions, holding S11 below −25 dB across 2.7–3.0 GHz to cover the full ODMR band around the zero-field splitting frequency.
  • Redesigned the board in Altium around the new antenna and a lower-phase-noise RF synthesizer to improve achievable spin coherence, and reworked the optical readout to improve fluorescence collection.
